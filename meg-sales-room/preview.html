Stage 2 · Step 2 of 3

Franchise Disclosure Document

The single most important document you will read in this process. Federal law requires us to give it to you.

The Franchise Disclosure Document, or FDD, is a 23-item disclosure that every franchisor in the United States must provide. Its contents are set by the Federal Trade Commission, not by us.

Read all of it. Then have your attorney and your accountant read it too. That is not a formality, and candidates who skip it regret it.

The 14 day rule protects you

You must have the FDD in hand for at least 14 calendar days before you sign any agreement or pay us any money. That waiting period is federal law.

Signing the receipt starts that 14 day clock. It does not commit you to anything, and it is not an agreement to buy a franchise.

What signing the receipt means

Signing the FDD receipt confirms one thing only: that you received the document on a given date.

It is not a purchase, a commitment, or an agreement of any kind.

Item 19 - Financial Performance Representations

Item 19 is the only place a franchisor may make a financial performance representation, or FPR. If a number about revenue or profit is not in Item 19, we cannot give it to you.

Franchisors are not required to include an Item 19. Many do not. The absence of one is normal and is not a red flag by itself.

Item 20 - your validation list

Item 20 lists current franchisees and, separately, owners who have left the system in the past year. Contact information is included.

That list is yours to use. You do not need our permission to call anyone on it, and we do not choose who you speak with. Stage 3 is built around those conversations.

Owner Calls

One of the most exciting steps in the Mutual Evaluation Process is connecting with owners.

Read the acknowledgment below before your first call. It explains a rule that governs every financial conversation in this process.

Required acknowledgment

I understand that my Franchise Developer can only speak to revenue and profitability numbers stated in the Franchise Disclosure Document Item 19. Any other information regarding aspects of financial performance must be obtained through conversations with franchise owners.

Why that rule exists

Federal law limits what a franchisor may say about financial performance. The only sanctioned place for those figures is Item 19 of the Franchise Disclosure Document, or FDD.

Your Franchise Developer, or FD, is not dodging you when they decline a revenue question. They are following a rule that protects you from unverified claims.

Franchise owners are under no such restriction. They can tell you what they actually experience, and that is exactly why these calls matter.

Funding

Four paths lead into this business. Most owners use one, some combine two.

Funding is the conversation candidates most want to skip and most regret skipping. Work it now, while you still have every option open.

Where we draw the line

We will not award a franchise to people who may be put in financial peril.

That is not a formality. If the numbers say a candidate would be stretched past a safe point, we say no, and we say it early.

Two questions before we go further

Both answers stay between you and your Franchise Developer, or FD. They set the size of the reserve you need beyond the cost of opening.

Required - self assessment

What are your combined total Personal Monthly Household Responsibilities?

Required - self assessment

What percentage of those expenses is covered by outside sources?

Outside sources means income that continues whether or not the business produces anything. A spouse's salary, a pension, or rental income all count.

SBA lending

Most franchise lending runs through the Small Business Administration, or SBA. The agency does not lend directly. It guarantees part of a bank's loan, which makes banks willing to lend against a business rather than only against collateral.

The brand's SBA loan history is a matter of public record. Across 12 loans, the charge-off rate is 0.0%. That figure describes how those loans have performed for lenders. It is not a representation about what any business earns.

Every SBA applicant files a Personal Financial Statement, or PFS. Fill it out once, carefully, and it will serve every lender you approach.

ROBS

Rollovers as Business Startups, or ROBS, lets you fund a business with retirement savings without triggering an early withdrawal penalty or income tax at the time of the rollover.

It is a legitimate and well established structure. It is also strict, and it must be set up by a specialist firm.

  1. Form a C corporation for the new business.
  2. The corporation sponsors a new retirement plan.
  3. Roll your existing retirement funds into that new plan.
  4. The plan purchases stock in the corporation, and the corporation now holds the cash.

Self funding

Self funding uses cash and liquid assets you already hold. No lender, no debt service, and no approval timeline.

The tradeoff is your reserve. Putting every liquid dollar into opening leaves nothing to absorb a slow first quarter.

HELOC

A Home Equity Line of Credit, or HELOC, borrows against equity in your home. It is usually a supplement to another path rather than the whole plan.

Understand the risk plainly. A HELOC puts your home behind the business.

Do not forget working capital

The cost of opening is not the cost of operating. You need enough cash to cover the business and your household until revenue is steady.

That reserve is what the two questions at the top of this page are sizing.

Territory

Where you operate shapes everything else. Territory decisions are effectively permanent once agreements are signed.

Three things to settle on this page: how territories are designed, how your local business name has to look, and the physical address requirement.

How territories are designed

A territory is not just a circle on a map. It is drawn around the variables that actually drive emergency plumbing demand.

Commercial emergency plumbing is not. The relevant variables are likely commercial building count, property-management concentration, permit volume, and drive-time to an emergency call. Getting this wrong compounds, and territory decisions are permanent once agreements are signed.

The DBA smell test

Your local entity will operate under a Doing Business As name, or DBA. That name and its address have to look like a real local business to a search engine, because that is how customers find you in an emergency.

This is Google's world, and we just live in it.

Someone with a burst pipe does not browse. They search, they call the first credible result, and they are done in ninety seconds. If your listing looks questionable, you never enter that decision.

The physical address requirement

You need a unique physical address inside your territory. This is not a brand preference. It is what business listing verification requires.

Verification is done by mailing a postcard with a code to the address on the listing. A mailbox service cannot receive that postcard as a distinct business location, and a P.O. box cannot either.

Unique matters too. If another business is already verified at your address, your listing competes with it rather than standing on its own.

Required acknowledgment

I understand that I will need a unique physical address within my territory, and that P.O. boxes and shared mailbox services do not qualify.

Sort the address early. Candidates who leave it to the week before opening find that verification takes longer than they planned.

Sample agreements

You will receive sample agreements alongside the Franchise Disclosure Document, or FDD. They are attached as exhibits to the FDD itself.

Sample means unsigned and unfilled. They show you exactly what you would be asked to sign, with your specific terms left blank. Give them to your attorney now, not later.

The e-signature walkthrough

Documents route to you through DocuSign. The email you receive uses DocuSign's standard language, and that language causes real confusion.

Watch for a DocuSign email from your Franchise Developer. It is legitimate, and it is not phishing.

Acknowledgment is not execution

The DocuSign email will say "Begin Signing Process." That wording is DocuSign's, not ours.

This is only an acknowledgment of receipt. It is not execution.

You are confirming you received the documents on a given date. You have not bought anything, you have not committed to anything, and you can still walk away.

Execution happens in Stage 6, after board approval, on a separate set of documents, with your FD walking you through it. You will know when it is real.

Contingencies

Contingencies are the conditions that must clear before you open. Each one gets a date and an owner on the DAP.

Typical items include funding approval, entity formation, your territory address, insurance, and licensing. Your FD sets the specific list with you.

Build in slack. Lending timelines and municipal licensing both move slower than candidates expect.

What / Who to Expect at Meet The Team Day

You are meeting the people who will support your business for years, not a sales team.

Meet The Team Day, or MTTD, puts you in a room with the operations team. These are the people you will call when a technician quits, when a job goes wrong, or when you want to open a second territory.

Learn their names and what each one owns. It makes your first year easier.

What the day is for

Three things, in this order.

  • You evaluate us. Ask hard questions of the people who will answer your calls later.
  • We evaluate fit. Leadership is forming a view on whether we can support you well.
  • You see the operation. Systems, training, and support look different in person than on a call.

How to get value out of it

Come with questions written down. The day moves fast and the ones you meant to ask are the ones you forget.

Ask about failure, not just success. Ask what happens when an owner falls behind, and what support actually looks like in that situation.

Post Meet The Team Day Assessment

Complete this while the event is fresh. Same week, ideally the same day.

Your Franchise Developer, or FD, reads your responses before your debrief call. That is what makes the debrief useful rather than generic.

Memory fades fast and softens edges. The reservation you felt clearly on Thursday is hard to name by the following Wednesday.

Stage 6 · Step 1 of 4

Executive Board Approval

The final decision, and the one your Franchise Developer does not get a vote in.

Your file goes to the executive board. The board reviews everything from Stage 2 forward and decides whether to award a franchise.

Your Franchise Developer, or FD, presents your case. Your FD does not vote. That separation is deliberate, and it is what keeps this a real review rather than a rubber stamp.

Three questions before your FD presents

Your FD has to be able to answer yes to three questions before your file goes forward at all.

SRC The three yes questions are pending. The source lesson carried them specifically. Restore the exact three rather than approximating, because they are the FD's own gate on whether a file is ready.

Three possible outcomes

Executive board outcomes
OutcomeWhat it meansWhat happens next
Full ApprovalThe board awards the franchise with no outstanding conditions.You move directly to executing your agreement.
Conditional ApprovalThe board awards the franchise once specific conditions are met.Your FD works the conditions with you, then the file returns to the board.
Not ApprovedThe board does not award a franchise at this time.Your FD walks you through the reasoning.

Not Approved is a healthy outcome

It does not feel that way, and it is still true.

Why the board says no

We will not put people in financial peril.

If the board's read is that a candidate would be stretched past a safe point, the answer is no. That answer protects the candidate more than it protects us.

A no is sometimes about timing rather than about you. Candidates come back later with a stronger position and are awarded then.

Ask your FD for the reasoning. You are entitled to understand it.
