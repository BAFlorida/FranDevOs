Investment Case Design System

The system for anything that goes to Riverside: capital requests, brand growth plans, add-on theses, and the recurring board review. Neutral field, brand in the accent slot, every figure sourced in the rail. One token layer renders to HTML, PDF, and PPTX.

01

Color

The field is neutral. Brand blue and teal appear on data, gates, and the claim mark only — never as a page background. This is what separates a board document from a marketing deck. The data ramp steps monotonically in lightness so every series stays distinct on a black-and-white printer, which is how half the committee will read it.

02

Type

Three faces, three jobs. Archivo carries claims — engineered and squarish, it reads like a nameplate on a machine, which suits a platform called Smith. Source Serif carries prose, because a PE reader reads memos, and serif body signals argument rather than pitch. IBM Plex Mono carries every number, label, and source line, so figures align in a column and stay legible in a table.

03

The evidence rail

The signature element, and the only structural rule in the system. Every surface carries a 104px mono rail: claim number at the top, a source line for every figure in the body, document status pinned at the foot. You cannot lay out a page in this system without filling it. Sourcing stops being a habit you remember and becomes a constraint the grid enforces. On mobile the rail collapses to a stacked block — it never disappears.

04

Claim headline

The headline is the argument, not a topic label. A committee member who reads only the headlines should be able to reconstruct the whole case. This is the Pyramid Principle enforced as a type style: if the headline can be true or false, it is a claim. If it can only be a subject, it is a label.

Lead Generation · Q3

Paid cost per qualified lead fell 78% while qualified volume held flat

Banned headlines

Q3 Lead Performance — a topic. Tells the reader nothing.

Marketing Update — a filing category, not an argument.

Key Takeaways — if they were key, they would be the headline.

Scenario cards

Base, upside, downside. The downside card is never omitted. A case without a downside is a pitch, and an operating partner will read its absence as either naivety or concealment. Showing the downside first is how you buy the room's trust for the base case.

07

Gate track

Capital releases against milestones, not against the calendar. The numbering is legitimate here because the sequence is real and the order carries information the reader needs. A gate track is also the single most effective de-risking device in a capital request: it lets the committee say yes to the first tranche without saying yes to all of it.

10

Risk register

Every risk carries a named owner and a mitigation that is already funded or already scheduled. A risk without an owner is a disclaimer, and a committee reads disclaimers as things you have not thought about.

RiskSeverityMitigationOwner
SBA credit tightening stalls qualified candidates at financing High Pre-qualify against lender criteria at stage 3, not stage 9. Two-lender panel in place. FranDev
Attribution gaps across seven brand portals distort lead cost Medium Single object model, Gate 1 deliverable. Funded. RevOps
Broker channel mix shifts candidate quality downward Low Channel-level close rate tracked monthly. Spend re-allocated at threshold. FranDev
11

Callout

The staged reveal. Once per deck. Never twice. Its power is entirely a function of scarcity — use it on the second callout and you have taught the room to skim past the first.

The dashboard shows lead cost falling. It does not show that the leads getting cheaper are the ones that never close.
